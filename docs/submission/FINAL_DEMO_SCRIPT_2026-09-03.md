# LabSpace Atlas — final demo runbook

Target: **2:50–2:58**, 1920×1080, 100% browser zoom, Balanced rendering. The video should show a
real running product for nearly its entire duration. Render should be warmed before recording.

## Why this order

The story escalates through three distinct WebMCP jobs instead of listing features:

1. **Build** proves structured physical creation from natural language.
2. **Stock** proves a mutation that still stops for a human decision.
3. **Find the work** proves cross-room evidence, honest missing stock and a spatial handoff.

This mirrors the strongest Build Week demos: name a painful real job, show one coherent working
loop, make the trust boundary visible, and end on evidence rather than a feature catalogue.

## Recording order and narration

### 0:00–0:09 — Problem montage

**Screen:** Three fast original/AI-generated shots: a researcher compares a paper layout with a
crowded room; searches cabinet labels; then pauses over an incomplete reagent checklist. Cut to the
LabSpace Atlas wordmark. Do not show third-party logos, identifiable people or legible fake data.

**Voice:** “A laboratory can own the right room, equipment and reagents—and still lose time because
none of them share the same map.”

### 0:09–0:25 — Product and trust boundary

**Screen:** Real LabSpace Atlas, `LAB-D-00`, R-001/R-002. Open WebMCP. Show **24 tools ready** and
**Reviewed**. Human selects **Fast Draft** for the additive room build.

**Voice:** “LabSpace Atlas turns the laboratory into a structured spatial operating system. A
browser agent gets twenty-four semantic tools, while the researcher keeps the execution boundary.”

### 0:25–1:08 — Build R-003

**Screen:** Choose **Create R-003 from one request → Copy + show workspace**. Show the browser agent
calling `labspace_search_assets`, `labspace_create_room`, `labspace_plan_room`,
`labspace_stage_room_plan` and `labspace_audit_room`. Cut idle model time, but do not alter the call
order or result. Hold on the synchronized 2D/3D room and hosted openings.

**Voice:** “From one request, the agent discovers canonical assets, creates Researcher Office
R-003, calculates exactly thirty-eight square metres, hosts the door and two three-panel windows on
real walls, pairs three chairs to three desks, and audits the completed room. Fast Draft applies
only this validated first build, remains undoable, and was enabled by me—not by the agent.”

### 1:08–1:36 — Stock with human approval

**Screen:** Reopen WebMCP, select **Stage two enzyme records**. Show the proposal card with names,
quantities, ISO expiry dates and unassigned location warning. Click **Approve inventory** yourself.
Wait for **Saved in this browser**.

**Voice:** “Inventory is different. The agent stages alpha-glucosidase and lipase with exact
quantities and expiry dates, but it cannot invent a shelf or approve the change. I review the facts
and commit both records.”

### 1:36–2:30 — Find stock, expose what is missing, finish at the work surface

**Screen:** Select **Ground a DPPH collection**. Show brief search/inspection evidence across R-001
and R-002. Hold on **Chloroform — missing**. Start the collection guide, advance two or three stops,
then jump to the final R-002 work-surface highlight and exact evidence inspector.

**Voice:** “For a researcher-approved DPPH checklist, Atlas grounds the DPPH reagent, methanol,
both pipette-tip sizes, pipettes and the microplate reader against the live index. Chloroform is not
in stock, so it stays missing—no hallucinated substitute. After I confirm the records, the spatial
guide visits the exact locations and finishes at a real clear work surface. It is evidence for a
researcher, not an invented protocol or safety approval.”

### 2:30–2:48 — Proof and extensibility

**Screen:** Reopen WebMCP → Evidence. Expand one build event, one human inventory approval and one
workflow event. Click **Export proof**. Briefly show **More judge workflows**, including the LLE
solvent check.

**Voice:** “Every call, bounded result and human decision becomes exportable session evidence.
Judges can reuse the same tools for annexes, placement, resizing, LLE stock checks and exact spatial
search.”

### 2:48–2:58 — Close

**Screen:** Clean 3D R-003/R-002 view and LabSpace Atlas wordmark.

**Voice:** “LabSpace Atlas: design the lab, index every location, and let humans and agents work
from the same physical truth.”

## Capture method

- Use a clean browser profile so the public fixture begins with only R-001 and R-002.
- Wake the Render service and complete one throwaway rehearsal before the recorded take.
- Keep the browser-agent conversation and LabSpace in one window; hide private tabs and bookmarks.
- Record one honest live run. Remove waiting/dead air in the edit and disclose cuts with a small
  “idle time removed” caption; never splice a different result into a tool call.
- Keep cursor motion calm. Hold each result for at least 1.5 seconds; use 150–200% punch-in crops
  only when a tool name, missing-stock result or approval boundary must be read.
- Keep generated imagery to the opening nine seconds. The product—not the montage—must win the demo.
- Record narration separately after the screen cut so it stays calm, humane and under three minutes.
